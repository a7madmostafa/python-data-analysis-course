Mini EDA: where does the shop lose money?¶

This chapter puts the charts of this part to work on one business question about the NileCart sales table:

Some order lines lose money. Which ones, and why?

The charts are not explained again; the point is the order of the questions and why each chart was picked. The analysis goes from simple to rich:

  1. Univariate: one column at a time. What does it look like?
  2. Bivariate: two columns. What moves with what?
  3. Multivariate: three or more. Is it really one story?
  4. Conclusion: the answer, and what to do about it.

The chart follows from the columns in the question:

The question is about Chart
One number Histogram
One category Sorted bars
A number across categories Bars or box plot
Number vs number Scatter, or a correlation heatmap for many
A number over time Line
A number across two categories Heatmap

2. Bivariate: two columns¶

Question: Which numeric columns move together? Chart: correlation heatmap. It screens every pair at once and shows where to look first.

📌 Insight: discount and profit: only -0.22, a weak link. That is not a reason to drop discounts from the analysis: one number for all products can hide what happens inside each category.

Question: Which categories actually bring the money? Chart: sorted bars of margin. Margin is profit as a share of revenue, so a 60 EGP notebook and a 32,000 EGP laptop stand on the same scale.

3. Multivariate: three columns or more¶

Question: How does margin change with category and discount together? Chart: heatmap, a number across two categories. Margin keeps the categories comparable, and center=0 turns any loss red.

In [13]:
t = sales.groupby(["category", "discount"])[["profit", "revenue"]].sum()
pt = (t["profit"] / t["revenue"]).unstack()

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 3.5))
sns.heatmap(pt, annot=True, fmt=".0%", cmap="RdYlGn", center=0, cbar=False, ax=ax)
ax.set(title="Only one cell loses money", xlabel="Discount", ylabel="")
plt.show()

📌 Insight: here is the hidden pattern. Every category stays profitable at every discount except Electronics at 20%, the only negative cell (-3%). The weak -0.22 was an average over categories that behave very differently.

Question: Is it every line in that cell, or a few bad ones? Chart: scatter of revenue vs profit, one dot per line. hue adds the discount, and one panel per category keeps the scales honest.

4. Conclusion¶

A decision maker needs one page, not every chart, so the summary keeps only the three that carry the answer.

The answer

  • Which lines lose money? Electronics at a 20% discount: all 32 loss lines, across all four products, and nothing else.
  • Why? Electronics have a thin margin, and 20% off takes most of those lines below cost. The discount does not raise the quantity per line either.
  • When? Any month, but White Friday is the peak: 14 of the 32 are in the two Novembers.

Recommendation: cap Electronics at 10% and keep the White Friday deals on everything else. Those 43 lines earned -75,360 EGP. At 10% they would earn about 194,870 EGP, a gain of 270,230 EGP, or 5% of total profit.

How sure is this? The pattern is solid: it holds with only completed and shipped orders (19 loss lines, all Electronics at 20%). But the gain assumes those lines would still sell at 10%, and this data cannot show the customers who came only for the 20%. The cap should be tested on the next White Friday before it becomes the rule.

Note the path: the univariate step showed that losses exist, the bivariate step produced three suspects, and only the multivariate step showed they are one story.
