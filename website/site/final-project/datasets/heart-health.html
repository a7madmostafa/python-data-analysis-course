The data

Every year the US Centers for Disease Control (CDC) phones hundreds of thousands of adults and asks about their health: the BRFSS survey. This dataset is the 2022 survey, prepared as one table: one row per person, 40 questions.

It covers age, sex and state, habits (smoking, e-cigarettes, alcohol, physical activity, sleep), body measures (height, weight, BMI), and many conditions: heart attack, stroke, diabetes, asthma, depression, kidney disease…

No medical background is needed: almost every column is a simple answer like Yes, No or Former smoker.

About the format

  • The zip has three files. Use 2022/heart_2022_with_nans.csv, the version with missing values. Cleaning them is part of the project.
  • Don't use heart_2022_no_nans.csv (someone already deleted the rows with gaps) or the 2020 file (older and different columns).

Watch out for

These are hints, not instructions. Check each one in the data, decide what to do, and explain your choice in the notebook.
  • Missing values everywhere, from under 1% to almost 20% depending on the question. Which ones can you fill honestly, and which should stay missing?
  • BMI is missing for ~11% of the people, but some of them have a height and a weight…
  • Values that are hard to believe: 24 hours of sleep, a BMI near 100.
  • Long answer texts ("Current smoker - now smokes some days") need shorter labels before they fit on a chart.
  • HadDiabetes has four answers, two of them "half yes".
  • ⚠️ Age hides everything. Smokers, drinkers and inactive people have different ages. Compare groups inside the same age band.
  • A survey shows links, not causes. Some links may run backwards (people change habits after getting ill).

Questions to explore

Age and sex

  1. How does the heart-attack rate change with age? Is it different for men and women?

Habits

  1. Do smokers have more heart attacks than people who never smoked, at the same age? What about former smokers?
  2. Physical activity, alcohol, e-cigarettes: what do you see, and can you believe it?

Body and sleep

  1. Does BMI matter?
  2. Is there a "right" amount of sleep?

Other conditions

  1. Which conditions go together with heart attacks? What happens when people have several?

Places

  1. Which US states have the highest rates?
